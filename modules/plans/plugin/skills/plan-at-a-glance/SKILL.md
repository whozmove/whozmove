# Starting a plan at a glance

The user reads your plan in Whozmove's panel, which opens it on its "At a glance" block, with the rest folded under
it. Most people read that block and little else, so it has to tell the whole plan in one screen: what they'll see,
what they must decide, and what could go wrong. Start the plan with its title, then the block, in this shape:

```markdown
# Shopping list from saved recipes

## At a glance

- **What changes for you:** Add to List on any recipe builds one shopping list, grouped by aisle.
- **You decide:**
  1. Where the list lives: on your account (recommended), or only in this browser. Why: people plan on a laptop
     and shop on a phone.
  2. Different units of one item: shown side by side (recommended); or converted to grams and millilitres. Why:
     cups aren't grams.
- **Could go wrong:** about one saved line in five isn't read; it shows under Other, as written.
- **You'll try:** add two recipes with onions; the list has one line, "3 onions".
- **What it keeps or sends:** your list, on your account; nothing goes anywhere new.
- **Left out:** sharing, prices.
- **Before:** you copy ingredients out by hand. **After:** one list, by aisle.
```

## The rules

- **Each label exactly as above,** in bold, ending with a colon: the panel finds each slot by its label. Leave a slot
  out rather than fill it with something it isn't. What It Keeps or Sends only when the plan stores, reads or sends
  something new; Before and After only for a change people can see.
- **Every choice has your pick:** a numbered line, its question up to the first colon, the option you'd take marked
  "(recommended)", the others after ", or", then "Why:" and one line. When an option has a comma or an "or" in it,
  part the options with "; or" instead. The panel ticks your pick, and the user changes it with a press; keeping
  it means there's nothing to send back. At most four choices.
- **About 120 words in all, each slot at most two lines,** in plain words, as the user would say them: what they'll
  see, not how it's built. The panel never cuts it, so a long block is a long read.
- **Nothing here that the plan below doesn't say.** It's the plan, short, not a pitch.
- Then the plan as you'd write it, each section under a `##` heading. Its sections fold under the block, each to its
  heading and first sentence, so start each section with the sentence that says what it's about.

## Sent back

When the user sends the plan back, write the next version whole, with its block brought up to date.
